Recipe tags – the filter row, and the tap that applies one

Two problems, drawn 2026-08-20 at Thomas's request. First: what a filter row does when it outgrows one line. Then, added the same day once he chose it: the quick-tag on the recipe card — the affordance that has to exist before the heart can go.

These are Claude's sketches, not design. Grey pills on purpose — no DS tokens, no type ramp, no Figma. Nothing here is a decision, and the real chips are the DS Chip component that already ships in src/components/ui/chip.tsx.
Settled already, and it shapes all four: tags multi-select and combine with AND, and Favorites joins them in the same row instead of staying a radio. So any number of chips can be lit at once — which is exactly why a chip that is ON must never be the one that scrolls out of sight.

The cheapest build and the standard iOS pattern — App Store, Music and Photos all do it. Costs no vertical space at all, which matters in the add-meal sheet.

Good: nothing new to build, no height, scales to any number.
Bad: you cannot see what tags exist without swiping, so the family forgets its own vocabulary. And re-sorting active chips to the front means the row reshuffles under your thumb — the chip you just tapped jumps somewhere else.

One component, one cap per canvas: two lines on the recipes list, one line in the sheet. Below the cap it is option B and costs one tap. Above it, the last slot becomes an overflow chip opening option C's sheet. The family that makes five tags never sees the sheet exist; the family that makes twenty is not punished.

The honest cost: it is two surfaces, so it is the expensive answer — the same trap as the ingredient-alias sketch. If a real cookbook turns out to hold six tags, B alone is the right build and this is over-engineering.

This is the condition attached to the AND decision, not a nice-to-have. AND teaches itself while the list shrinks — thirty recipes become three and the meaning is obvious without a word. The moment it hits zero, that teaching stops and it reads as an empty cookbook or a broken screen.

So the empty state names both tags and offers a one-tap way out. It is also the most likely state in the add-meal sheet, where the library is small and the questions are specific — “quick and veggie, for Tuesday”.

Part two · the tap that applies a tag Thomas chose this, 2026-08-20

Why this frame has to exist: if Favorites becomes an ordinary tag, the heart goes — and the heart is one tap on the card, the most-used action in the app. Without a fast way to tag from the list, favouriting becomes a four-tap job and the whole idea is a downgrade. This affordance is what makes it a net gain instead: every tag becomes applicable without opening the recipe.
⚠️ The card is a HALF-WIDTH GRID TILE, and that is the constraint here. Photo, one line of title, a time — about 128pt across (recipe-card.tsx, Figma list section 78:4010). The heart worked because one glyph carries exactly one bit. Several tags do not fit on this card at all, which is the real design problem below, not the tap itself.

The glyph is a design call, not this sketch's. A luggage-tag shape reads as “tag” but is busy at 24pt; a bookmark reads as “saved”, which is nearly the old meaning and may be a feature. Whatever it is, it is one icon doing what a heart did.

Considered and rejected: long-press the card. No chrome at all, and nobody would ever find it — the same objection that killed drag-to-merge in the ingredient-alias sketch. It can exist as a shortcut; it cannot be the only way in.

One tap to open, one to tag, swipe away — and the recipe never had to open.

Lit glyph · count badge · one tag and an overflow. At 128pt the third is already at its limit, and it displaces the cooking time.

This is what the heart was quietly buying. One glyph, one bit, no layout question. A tag set is not one bit, and a half-width tile has nowhere to put it.

Claude's leaning: the lit glyph, and nothing more. It says “this one is tagged”, which is all the old heart said, and the answer to which tags is the filter row — that is what the filter row is for. The count badge looks informative and is not: “2” does not tell you it is the dessert you were hunting for.

The cost, said plainly: you lose at-a-glance “this is a favourite” across the grid. That is a genuine loss and it is the price of the tag model — worth being sure about before the heart goes.

The tags live here in full — the card only ever hinted at them.

The heart appears three times in the code, not once: the card, a button on the recipe screen, and a row in that screen's menu (recipes/[id].tsx). All three have to go together, or the app contradicts itself — and the menu row becomes Tags…, opening the same sheet as F2.

The recipe screen is also the one surface with room to show tags properly, which is the argument for the card staying quiet: the detail is one tap away and always was.

Still open after both parts

How many tags does a real family make?Nobody knows, and it decides B against D outright. Six means B; twenty means D. Worth a guess out loud before drawing either.
What order do the chips sit in?Alphabetical is predictable and builds muscle memory. Most-used-first is smarter and reshuffles the row between visits, which is the same disorientation A was marked down for. The same question applies inside the quick-tag sheet.
Is losing at-a-glance favourites acceptable?The grid stops showing WHICH recipes are loved. That is the real cost of retiring the heart, and it is a judgement, not a trade-off with a right answer.
What is the glyph?A tag shape, a bookmark, something else. It replaces an icon everyone already understands, so it is doing more work than it looks.
Where does a tag get RENAMED or DELETED?Anybody may do both (settled), but no screen here offers it. The quick-tag sheet is the obvious home and is not drawn that way yet.
Does the sheet row scroll away?The add-meal chip row rides in the list header today, so it scrolls off with the content. With a filter switched on, that hides the reason the list looks short.
Also considered and not drawn: tags only in the search field — type “dessert”, get the tagged recipes, no chip row beyond the two that ship today. It is free, and search will match tag names anyway. It is not drawn because it is not the feature: Thomas asked to tap a tag the way you tap a favourite, and typing is not tapping. Worth knowing it exists as the fallback if the row never earns its space.

Claude's leanings, recorded for whoever picks this up: D for the filter row, built so a small cookbook cannot tell it apart from plain wrapping — and if the answer to “how many tags” turns out to be six, build B and stop. F2 for the quick-tag, because the app already speaks in sheets and now has one height rule for all of them. A lit glyph and no count on the card.

And one warning that outranks all of them: the heart is live on the App Store build, and two people in one kitchen can be on different builds. Nothing in part two ships until the seeding and dual-write sequencing in the backlog item is done — otherwise one member's favourites quietly stop reaching the other's phone.
